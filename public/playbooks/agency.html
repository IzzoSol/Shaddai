The SHADDAI Playbooks · Vol. I

The Agency Scaling Playbook

How to turn a service business you run into a machine that runs — the offer, the leads, the pricing, the delivery, and the team.
Written by QUILL · SHADDAI's writing agent

Most agencies don't stall because the work is bad. They stall because the founder is the business — the offer, the sales, the delivery and the fires all run through one person. This playbook is about removing you as the bottleneck, one system at a time.

You'll notice these ideas rhyme with what the best operators — Alex Hormozi chief among them — teach for free. That's on purpose: the frameworks that actually scale service businesses are well-known. What's rare is applying them to your specific business in order. That's what this is.

01 · THE OFFERMake saying no feel stupid

Everything downstream — your leads, your prices, your margins — is decided by the strength of your offer. A weak offer means you compete on price and chase clients. A strong one means clients chase you.

Judge every offer on four levers. Raise the top two, shrink the bottom two:

  • Dream outcome — the specific result the client actually wants (not "marketing," but "a booked calendar").
  • Perceived likelihood — why they'll believe you can deliver it (proof, guarantees, a named process).
  • Time delay — how fast they see a win. Shorten it; engineer a fast first result.
  • Effort & sacrifice — how much work it is for them. Do more for them, not with them.

"We grow X for Y in Z days — or we work free until we do."

Name your outcome, put a timeframe on it, and back it with a guarantee you can survive. A guarantee isn't a discount — it's the single fastest way to raise perceived likelihood.

Your move
Write your offer as one sentence: "We help [who] get [dream outcome] in [time], guaranteed by [risk reversal]." If you can't, that's the work.

02 · THE NICHERiches are in the niches

The fastest way to raise all four offer levers at once is to narrow who you serve. A "marketing agency" is a commodity. "We fill dental practices with new patients" is a category of one.

Niching does three things: it makes your proof stack faster (same problem, repeatedly), it lets you charge more (specialists cost more than generalists), and it makes your marketing write itself (you know exactly who you're talking to).

  • Pick a niche you can reach (they gather somewhere) and that has money.
  • Decide who you won't serve. A clear "not for you" sharpens the "for you."
  • You can widen later. You cannot get famous being for everyone.

03 · LEAD GENERATIONThe four ways to get clients

There are only four ways to reach people, split by whether you know them and whether you pay. Master them in this order:

  1. Warm outreach — people who already know you. Fastest first clients. Message everyone relevant, offer help, don't pitch.
  2. Content — post free value to your niche daily. Give away the "what" and the "why"; sell the "done for you." Compounds forever.
  3. Cold outreach — strangers, one at a time. Personalized, high-volume, offer-led. Predictable if you do the reps.
  4. Paid ads — strangers, at scale. Turn ads on after the first three prove the offer converts, not before.

Do more of what's working, and better what isn't. That's the whole game.

The trap
Founders jump to paid ads to avoid the discomfort of outreach. Ads amplify an offer — they don't fix one. Prove it by hand first.

04 · SALESPrice the value, not the hours

Hourly pricing punishes you for getting good. Price the outcome. If you make a client $10k/month, $3k/month is a bargain — and the number has nothing to do with your hours.

The consult that closes is mostly listening. Diagnose the problem, quantify what it's costing them, then present your offer as the bridge. When they say "it's expensive," they mean "I don't yet believe it'll work" — answer with proof and risk reversal, not a discount.

  • Lead with questions; let them say the problem out loud.
  • Quantify the cost of not solving it.
  • Anchor high, then present the package.
  • Hold your price. Discounting your first clients trains your market to expect it.

05 · PRICING & PACKAGINGProductize the service

Custom everything doesn't scale. Turn your service into packages — named, fixed-scope, repeatable. Three tiers work: a Good, a Better (make this the obvious choice), and a Best that makes Better look reasonable.

  • Sell retainers, not one-offs — recurring revenue is what makes an agency an asset.
  • Raise prices on new clients first; you'll feel the fear and the math both.
  • Every productized package is one step closer to something you can delegate.

06 · DELIVERYSystematize so it scales without you

Growth dies in delivery. If every project lives in your head, every new client makes your life worse. The fix is boring and it works: write it down.

  1. Document the process — checklists and SOPs for how the work actually gets done.
  2. Templatize — reuse everything: onboarding, reporting, deliverables.
  3. Make results repeatable — consistent outcomes are what generate referrals and case studies.

A great result is your best salesperson. Engineer them on purpose.

07 · THE TEAMBuy back your time

You scale the day you stop doing the work and start owning the outcome. Hire in the order that removes you fastest:

  • First: delivery help — a contractor to do the work you've now documented.
  • Then: an account/ops lead to own clients and quality.
  • Last: sales, once the offer and delivery are proven and stable.

Delegate outcomes, not tasks. Give someone the checklist, the standard, and the authority — then let them own it.

08 · RETENTION & REFERRALSKeep and multiply

It's far cheaper to keep and expand a client than to win a new one. Churn is a silent killer — a leaky bucket no amount of new leads will fill.

  • Set expectations early — most churn is a communication failure, not a results failure.
  • Show the win — report outcomes in their language (revenue, bookings), not vanity metrics.
  • Expand accounts — the easiest next sale is to a happy client.
  • Ask for referrals at the peak — right after a visible win, not at renewal.

09 · THE 90-DAY MOVEWhere to start Monday

Don't do all of this at once. Do it in order:

  1. Days 1–30 — Offer & niche. Rewrite your offer as one sentence with a guarantee. Pick one niche. Say no to the rest.
  2. Days 31–60 — Leads & sales. Run warm + cold outreach and daily content by hand. Hold your new, higher price on every call.
  3. Days 61–90 — Delivery & first hire. Document your process. Make your first delivery hire against those SOPs. Build the referral ask into your delivery.

Now — don't just read it. Run it.
