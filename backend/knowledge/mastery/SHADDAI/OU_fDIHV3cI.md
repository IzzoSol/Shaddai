## THESIS
The Major Questions Doctrine serves as an essential check on administrative agencies by requiring explicit congressional authorization for significant regulatory actions.

## FRAMEWORKS & PRINCIPLES
- Legal principle demanding clear statutory authority from Congress before major agency decisions can be made.
- Flexibility over deference in the interpretation of statutes, especially under Chevron doctrine's application limits set forth since 1986.
- Degrees and interpretive flexibility within judicial review when determining what constitutes a "major question."
- The potential for ideological bias influencing how judges apply or reject this principle.
